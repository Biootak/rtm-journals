I like the idea of collecting probability statistics. I suppose it is good for providing some assurance while trading. I would suggest you to leave collecting numbers at the moment, and invest your efforts in defining good levels, when you'll be comfortable in collecting zones and levels, then your statistics may gain you the confidence for trading.

As for your DBR RBD. Please note that some zones you have marked are RBR/DBD. My idea of this assignment is to locate possible turning points, then see how it reacts when price test these levels.

Some levels that were "ignored" had prior signs. I would suggest you to look for DBR/RDB that are the strongest. you can define strong level by 2 criterias: 1. How strong was the away move >> then >> its a strong level why:: We assume that at the decision point of breaking the balance (among sellers and buyers) are left with stacks of un-filled orders. 2. How Long was the Price at the defined level before it unbalanced > The least is better. why:: As more time given for buyers and seller to negotiate, the more deals the close. The least time they have to negotiate, we assume for having more un-filled orders left at the zone.

There are more ways to define the quality of a level, you will get to learn these with the other HW assignments.
