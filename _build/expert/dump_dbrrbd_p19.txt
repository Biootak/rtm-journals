Take aways from looking at the DBR and RBD's:

1. When price moves away from a level, it doesnt matter how soon a re-test back into that comes, thats the highest probability trade because of the probability of unfilled orders resting there.

2. When price rallies back into a previous RBD, the supply that came from the D of that previous RBD was cause of a new area of supply to form that is slightly lower than the previous (often). This causes the lower market structure highs to form, because new levels of supply just below the previous market structure highs has formed, capping price from reaching higher levels. This works both ways, and is what seems to happen many times, not always.

3. Price seems clearer to me that it is always moving around to find piles of orders, and depending on the strength of moves away from those piles, we can reason better about what the probabilities are when price revisits and retests.

4. Wicks or tails mean at the point price reached that extent, there were S/D pushing price faster than the opposing direction, but then that move is overwhelmed by the orders that get touched and filled.

5. When resting orders sit at these levels, they only execute when market orders take them out, or other execution mechanics force those orders to move to market orders. It is still liquidity takers that must drive price through these levels.

6. When price breaks out of a recent high or base, it often returns on the other side and re-tests. Re-testing suggests a conscious act for price to go test a previous level. To me it seems like it is more a result of price bouncing away from supply and working its way back to value, but as it hits the demand that was strong enough to break those highs, thats were orders exist to give this level support. This is S/R in action.

I am sure theres much much more I have missed. But here is my next analysis.

برداشت‌هایی از نگاه به DBR و RBDها:

1. وقتی قیمت از یک لول دور می‌شود، مهم نیست retest به آن چقدر زود می‌آید؛ این بالاترین احتمال ترید است چون احتمال سفارش‌های پرنشده‌ای که آنجا مانده‌اند.

2. وقتی قیمت به یک RBD قبلی رالی می‌کند، supplyای که از D آن RBD قبلی آمده بود علت شکل‌گرفتن ناحیهٔ supply تازه‌ای شد که کمی پایین‌تر از قبلی است (اغلب). این باعث می‌شود سقف‌های ساختار بازار پایین‌تر شکل بگیرند، چون لول‌های supply جدید درست زیر سقف‌های قبلی ساختار بازار شکل گرفته‌اند و قیمت را از رسیدن به لول‌های بالاتر باز می‌دارند. این هر دو طرف کار می‌کند و به‌نظر می‌رسد بارها اتفاق می‌افتد، نه همیشه.

3. به‌نظرم قیمت همیشه برای یافتن پشته‌های سفارش جابه‌جا می‌شود، و بسته به قدرت حرکت‌ها از آن پشته‌ها، می‌توانیم بهتر دربارهٔ احتمال‌ها وقتی قیمت بازمی‌گردد و retest می‌کند استدلال کنیم.

4. wick یا tail به این معناست که در نقطه‌ای که قیمت به آن وسعت رسید، S/D قیمت را سریع‌تر از جهت مخالف هل می‌داد، ولی بعد آن حرکت با سفارش‌هایی که لمس و پر می‌شوند غرق می‌شود.

5. وقتی سفارش‌های resting روی این لول‌ها می‌نشینند، فقط وقتی market orderها آن‌ها را take out می‌کنند یا مکانیک اجرای دیگر آن سفارش‌ها را به market order تبدیل می‌کند اجرا می‌شوند. هنوز liquidity takerها هستند که باید قیمت را از این لول‌ها عبور دهند.

6. وقتی قیمت از یک سقف یا بیس اخیر breakout می‌کند، اغلب از سمت دیگر برمی‌گردد و retest می‌کند. retest کردن به معنای یک عمل آگاهانه برای رفتن تست یک لول قبلی است. به‌نظرم بیشتر نتیجهٔ این است که قیمت از supply پرت می‌شود و راهش را به‌سمت value پیدا می‌کند، ولی وقتی به demandای می‌رسد که به‌اندازهٔ کافی قوی بود که آن سقف‌ها را بشکند، آنجا سفارشی هست که به این لول پشتیبانی می‌دهد. این S/R در حال عمل است.

Hello tradingjedi,

As well as identifying the RBDs and DBRs, you need to mark out at what level reactions occurred (if any), consider those that failed and what happened at the level afterwards. Maybe not to the same degree that mettafort and I have just done

- we had a

common interest but different perspectives to explore, hopefully to the benefit of everyone.

A long while back I was in a webinar and the speaker made an almost throw-away statement - which I'm sure sailed straight through most people, but which piqued my interest. It was in response to a question I don't recall, but the reply was, "Although we look at the same charts, we see different things". There was more to it than that, but pondering that statement allowed me to form many questions I didn't even know I needed to ask, then answer them to find more questions. It started me on a path of discoveries - still ongoing - that I doubt I would've walked otherwise. I won't expand beyond that, but what is interesting is that three people looked at roughly the same GBPUSD information and applied their logic to it: two of those people had entirely different opinions of the same RBD and one person ignored it altogether on their chart!

So, the benefit comes from the qualitative exercise:

1. What attracted you to this particular RBD or DBR?, what didn't you like? Was your opinion that it would work or not? (Your opinion, mind, not the benefit of 20:20 hindsight 2. What happened when price did return to the Base in each case? If it reacted, where (zoom in to the Base and root around)? If it didn't, is there anything that you can learn about it so you can spot a bad-un in the future (that's why we need to find good and bad examples)?

3. What happened later when price returned to the Base from the other side? Did it react or ignore it again? 4. Zoom out and look back in time, does that change your perception of your chosen examples or help understand why some worked or failed?
