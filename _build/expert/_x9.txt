To Harsh (I hope you dont mind IF):

Hey Harsh-

This is how I see it. RBR/DBR are formations, as well as metaphors.
These formations are what I see as canonical examples of something more
subtle - disequilibrium. The base of DBR is the area of focus, for it
has within it an area of equilibrium - the base. Price bounces tightly
in a state of balance, and then eventually price rises, fast or slow.
The way in which price leaves an area is what the focus is in the end as
 far as I see it - if price leaves slowly, the probability of orders
being left at the base area is lower than if price shoots away.

In IF's diagram the base is at the bottom of the PAZ. That is a DBR for
 sure, and the R piece engulfs the upper supply. The source of this
move then is the move away from the base. But notice also that price
pulls back and makes an FTR, which itself is a variant of a DBR. Again,
 when price leaves the FTR, if it moves well, it becomes an area of
significance when price revisits.

When price is seemingly stable, and then moves quickly away, this is
place to focus on. It is the Balance->Imbalance cycle. You can spot
 this all over a chart. A good example is at the extremes. Imagine
price breaking to new highs, and then struggle to get higher. Then a
ledge is formed as price falls - a type of base with perhaps the highs
falling but the lows remaining at the same level for 5 bars or so. Then
 price plummets, confirming supply is up here - this is one version of
what some refer to as a hook. The point being that price might not look
 like a canonical base, RBR, DBR, RBD, etc....the key is to look beyond
that, IMHO.

HTH,

MF.
