hopefully help you.

Starting with a blank chart:

1. Drawing your major lines, at the extremes, where price has crossed it
 at least once. These are the lines that cut across all the way the
charts. The obvious stuff. Use judgement call of how far to go back as
 your TFs get smaller. There shouldn't be too many of these.

2. This one is trickier and you'll just have to practice and find a way
that works for you. The tops and bottoms of FLs that broke an important
 FL are important. But when price crosses price alot, it's leads to a
lot of lines. So if it looks important, draw a line across the chart.
If you're unsure, maybe only if it's been crossed recently.

3. Include the rest: PCP, Cm breaks, etc...Again same as two, maybe only
 include recently crossed ones, unless it looks really important.

4. Any other areas that seem to stand out, given what price is currently doing.

5*. I'll also throw in areas where price swings are at least semi-major
 and tends to swing a lot, if it hasn't been picked up by any of the
above (which is unlikely). The area will need more 'touches' the lower
the timeframe. So for example, in Mn it needs at least 3 touches. In
Wk it needs at least 5 touches. etc... These numbers can change
relative to the chart (charts that whipsaw back and forth will naturally
 have alot of touches. For example USDJPY vs USDCAD in Mn chart.

These should be all your major lines. For lines that you are not sure
about, just leave it out for now. I'll naturally be picked up in a lower
 tf. Then go and find your kinks. If you're not sure about something,
just use a judgement call. Don't worry too much about being wrong.
