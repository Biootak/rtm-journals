Hey, Mr L. The easiest way to differentiate is that price exiting an FTR must engulf something significant in the past - this is represented by the red lines on the original article's FTR diagrams. RBRs and DBDs only engulf themselves, if you get me.

The FTR's move away engulfing something tells you that it had a strong SD imbalance, and so is a good opportunity on the return. The DBD/RBR on the other hand does not, and so is better as a target than an entry point.

Now, the place where I need to have more understanding is where we have a flag occur after breaking an SR area. It's a RBR/DBD more than an FTR, and yet is a good place to take an entry on the return, the teaching states (flag limit article) but I beleive that a flag forming prior to the break should be more meaningful, as it also holds the decision point to break the SR area, and thus holds a greater S/D imbalance.
