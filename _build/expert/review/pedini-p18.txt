This is how I was studying it, hopefully it highlights the importance of the answers to those questions!

I have trouble believing in 'ancient' areas of Supply or Demand on longer time-frames. It's not that price doesn't turn at them sometimes,
 it's just that the orders which cause the reaction to the area are not
the remainder of those that created the original area - so if price
turns because of new orders, how important is the old area?

Too often I've sat waiting for a turn from an 'ancient' area, when none
has been forthcoming, and current PA has been telling me something
different (and that is not to say my reading of PA is any good
 ). To me, the importance and definition of areas decays over time
until they become either matters of convenience / coincidence, or traps
(for S / D traders!). So, with that in mind, I waited to see whether
price would react to the area, then looked for something in the shadow
of that reaction.

Also the Compression into the area might not indicate weakness, so I am
not anticipating a turn based on this leading confirmation: as there are
 fresh areas of Supply, I want to see them taken out first before I'm
convinced.
