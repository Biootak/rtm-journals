Caps on price

Every S/D trader knows them in and out, yet the best, strongest and perfect looking ones still fail.

I can conclude from my own experience that the shape of the base and
departure strength from a level have only limited influence on the
overall outcome.

What really matters, as RTM teaches, is the context.

But before I get into that. There are some important clues that I
consider to determine the quality of the base, I'm sure someone else
here at RTM pointed them out.

An interesting fact is that many traders think that consolidation (bases
 in RBD/RBD/RBR/DBD) means low activity (volume) and that momentum bars
happen due to high volume. Sure, sometimes this is right, but most of
the time momentum bars (liquidity gaps) are thin market moves (low
volume) because price has to move in one direction once the last
unfilled order is filled and ready to move to the next liquidity zone
(base), where enough opposing orders get filled to stop price for some
time, until again the balance gets broken one way or the other.

This is also the reason why price bounces from those bases, because of unfilled orders left over from the last battle.

S/D traders say that those orders at the bases are left there for undefined time, even for years.

But it was a real revelation to me what RTM teaches about targets (PAZ).

Another common reason for RBD/DBR failures is also explained by RTM.

Just by knowing this and the above, we should be able to filter out many losses.
