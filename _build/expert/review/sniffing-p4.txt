### sniffing_p4_8
EN: I've posted this in another topic and thought its time to bring it here.

Supply and Demand zones presents a stack of unfilled orders either offers or bids.

Stacked zones presents stacked groups of unfilled orders (limit orders), in every zone there are unfilled stacked orders on top of each other.

As Sam Seiden explained in a webinar - Unfilled orders moves market prices, the chart line/candles/bars represent filled/executed orders and the white space around it represent the unfilled orders (limit orders) thus we need to focus on the big unfilled orders (limit orders).

What I'll explain here based on my understanding of the market, so is the attached chart. The following example applies to the stacked zones as well as to stacked orders inside a zone.

In case of a supply zone scenario, offers shall be stacked above each other (the wider the zone the more orders at different prices) logically the majority would sell high and this is why unfilled orders would increase higher into the zone, and with every return of price to the zone the sell orders are decrease/consumed.....now this doesn't mean the smart or big money would be sitting at the highest prices....nope not necessarily....they are the smart money and they know where to set and distribute their offers...well, it could be among the highest offers and "for example" this is where a compression ends, or it might be among the lowest offers and this is when we see a spike or a quick touch of the zone's lower edge then price bounce and drops for a long distant before returning back and starts pressuring or forming ascending triangle to consumes the entire zone.

Of course the strength of buyers and sellers plays a big role in this process every time it happens, thus comes the importance of how did the price leave the zone, and how is price returning to the zone.

I hope I made it simple and clear.

P.S. The attached chart present Buy/Sell limit orders.
