Now onwards to FTRs. I know FTR article is removed, but it was a great exercise during my first time around to understand what a price bounce was a reaction to.

From the markepedia: FTRs: P: The charts are full of FTRs, learn what it is and how to identify it.

W: Flag limits and FTRs are the same, but not every FTR is a Flag Limit. Practice and learn how to use them in your trading.

From previous homeworks, bro Staurum actually gave out a nice hint on this already: without FTR, there is no FL without break there is no FTR without a zone or price barrier, there is no break without price movement, there is no zone or price barrier So I started with SRs that seems obvious up to the monthly level and worked my FTRs from those.

I marked all FTRs from the weekly timeframe and moved on to the daily timeframe.

The most important part of this exercise is to find the important barrier and the resulting FTR that would be created after.

Below is what I have marked for this exercise. Please note that not all FTRs are marked to prevent a mess of colors.

I just marked the important ones that are created after a break of significant SR or after a break of previous FTR.

My current understanding of FTRs: - FTRs can be seen all over the chart. To eliminate the worst ones, immediately remove the ones that broke local SRs, i.e. the ones created after the break of a previous highs or lows in the same swing. - FTRs can be created by a break of any barrier, but the most important ones are the FTR after the break of FTRs that held price up or down previously, which we all know as Flag Limits.

- FTRs are created by the ITs to either: trap retailers by creating false SD zones or mark their and their IT friends' PAZs.

- For the fake SD FTR, from IF's recent diagram explaining FTRs and FLs, these can be seen in FTRs thatdid not retrace to pick up the orders in the previous kink/FL. ITs create these FTRs to create liquidity from the SD traders, i.e. their stop losses for ITs to trade in the true SD. BSZ concept also comes from this fake SD concept of FTRs - For the true PAZ marker / PAZ delimiter type of FTR, I think there was a different concept in the beginning years of RTM, which causes a lot of confusion in the past. The new concept is that the more hidden the delimiter FTR, the better. as the retailers can't see them. This is what I understand currently: - The way we use FTRs now is to use them as fake SD zones to expect FO from. The most common way of tricking the retailers are where price reacted off the mouth of an important FTR (the FL), creating a fake SD zone (the FTR of the FL) and then on the next visit fakes to the MPL of the important FTR. Bro H recently wrote a lovely piece on thishere.

- Remember that all FLs are FTRs, but not all FTRs are FLs. Again, the distinction should be clear from IF's diagram above.

- After reading many of bro Salivan's journal, I also believe that FTRs that held price up or down many times are the important ones to look out for.

These would be saved later for the FL homework. Orderflow-wise, as in everyday buying and selling in any market, I think it makes the most sense for ITs to open their positions in roughly the same price level or only a few pips off rather than scattering their orders for a certain direction in a wider zone.

Thank you all for any corrections and comments. Have a lovely weekend
