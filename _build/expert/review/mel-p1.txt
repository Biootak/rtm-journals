Yup, there is a big difference between fighting price and following it.
For me, the best way to follow price is by chunking it down and trying
to see it as simply as I can. Watching the Hs, Ls, the ranges that form
from them and the movement in and around those ranges. For me boxes are
the foundation to my trading as they put a frame to what I am viewing.

What most learn about trading is levels and PA. The bigger piece of the
picture is how price MOVES. To get a feel for this, open up any chart on
 the 1h and get a pen and paper out. Draw out the main Hs and Ls. Then
draw out boxes to chunk it into individual bits. Look at how price moves
 in and around those Hs / Ls and the boxes. You will see that price has a
 few standard characteristics and they are incredibly simple. Usually
when price does 'A' it then does 'B'. For example, if price establishes a
 H/L with a retest, it will usually fill the range back or beyond the
previous H/L. These things are not patterns, they are dynamics.

Hs and Ls are the most essential part of the chart. Every day there is a
 H and L that is formed and there are many Hs and Ls that form in
between them.

Again with your pen and paper, divide the price out in time periods and
see how they move during different sessions and days of the week. Get
curious.. why does it do that?

It took me a few weeks of doing this to get to grips with it.

EN: Trading Plan:

Why am I a Trader?

I have been introduced to trading in 2008, didn't had any succes in the first years.

I didn't want to become a Trader when I was a little girl, in fact I
didn't even know that this kind of activity existed before. I guess, it
was the lack of succes that I had in the first years that was what kept
me going. This is what triggered my intrest to get to know more about
the market. I had this feeling that it was possible to become succesfull
 in trading and to learn everything about how the market worked, inside
 out. It has been a long journey since then, as I tested so many
different things that did not worked. In fact, it was me, that did not
knew how to read the market. Eventually, after so many years I found a
way, I finally discovered the true beauty of the market and now that I
know,I am certainly not going to give up and stop trading.... :)

Trading Style & Strategy

I consider myslef to be a Day/Swing Trader but I also take some scalps here and there if a good opportunity present itself.

I am a supply and demand trader but I also look at PA. I do a weekly
analysis every sunday for the coming week and I also adjust or modify my
 plan if needed every day, before London open.

I like to trader higher TF supply and demand zones but I do like to look for lower TF fresh zones within the higher TF ones.

I mainly trade the EU but I may also check other pairs once in awhile.

I do not trade the Asian session. I prefer to trade from London open untill London close.

Money Management:

I will never risk more then 2% per trade. If I do have 3 losses in a
row, I will stop trading for the day and I will analyze why the trades
failed.

-My r:r per trade need to be at least 1/1 , otherwise I will not take
the trade. If I take a trade at a supply zone, I would prefer to exit at
 the demand zone and vice versa.

-For my stop loss, I use 2% risk but I will look at LTF charts to look
for the last 2 decision points, if thos are broken (against me) then I
will exit the trade manually and I will take the loss.

-My main focus is to preserve my capital.

-In terms of return, my daily minimum goal is 20 pips gain. I normally
do not want to set a fix minimum return in terms of pips because I want
to take what the market wants to give me (if it's more fine, if less
fine too) but since I have to set some goals for myself, I won't go for
too much pips. 20 pips seems to be fair enough.

Points to be careful about :

-Don't trade what you think, but trade what you see. Buy at demand and sell at supply!

-Pay attention to higher TF zones, as those are more powerfull than LTF ones.

-Always try to improve yourself!

Explain how you do your analysis and what you look for.

1)Look at where price is NOW, go ahead and mark all the supply zones
that are above current price and all the demand zones that are under
current price on 1h up to a monthly chart.

2) Mark in a specific color which of this higher TF zones are Fresh ones.

3) Find lower TF fresh supply and demand zones that are located in the higher TF zones from point 1 & 2.

4)Now that you have the zones marked take another chart and mark last day, week and month highs and lows.

5) Back to point 1, 2 & 3. On a lower TF, identify where price is
now and try to locate where the previous decision points are (to the
upside and to the downside).

6) Pre-entry, when price does approach the zones that you have marked,
look at how it approches exactly. Is it compressing towards the supply
or demand zone? If yes, then this is a plus point.

7) Look at point 6, if we are approaching a supply for example, look at
where you first "problem area " can be. Is the distance between the
supply and the "problem area" at least 1/1 rr? If yes, then ok, if not
pass the trade. Look also at decision point for your stops, make sure it
 fits your stop size.

 About the entry,I want to look at PA. My prefered entry is this:

Let's assume we are in a higher TF supply zone, I like to nr 1 see price
 cp towards it at least on a LTF chart, then usually price start to flag
 under this supply ( on a LTF) , what I like to see is a move away from
this flag, then a break a minor demand base (on a LTF) and a retest of
the fresh formed supply flag.

Let's assume I took a similar trade, the next thing I want to do in
order to stay in the trade is to look for break of the next minor demand
 lows/decision point.If price is unable to break the lows, then it might
 move in the opposite direction, so I'll look to exit the trade.

I think that this is it. I will update this post if somthing changes in my trading plan.

توضیح بده تحلیلت را چطور انجام می‌دهی و دنبال چه چیزی می‌گردی.

2) با یک رنگ مشخص علامت بزن کدام‌یک از این نواحی تایم‌فریم بالاتر تازه‌اند.

3) نواحی عرضه و تقاضای تازهٔ تایم‌فریم پایین‌تر را پیدا کن که داخل نواحی تایم‌فریم بالاتر بند 1 و 2 قرار دارند.

4) حالا که نواحی را علامت زده‌ای، نمودار دیگری بردار و سقف و کف روز، هفته و ماه گذشته را علامت بزن.

5) برگرد به بندهای 1، 2 و 3. در یک تایم‌فریم پایین‌تر مشخص کن قیمت حالا کجاست و تلاش کن جای نقاط تصمیم قبلی را پیدا کنی (هم سمت بالا و هم سمت پایین).

6) پیش از ورود، وقتی قیمت به نواحی علامت‌خورده نزدیک می‌شود، ببین دقیقاً چگونه نزدیک می‌شود. آیا به سمت ناحیهٔ عرضه یا تقاضا فشرده می‌شود؟ اگر بله، این یک نکتهٔ مثبت است.

7) به بند 6 نگاه کن؛ اگر مثلاً به یک عرضه نزدیک می‌شویم، ببین اولین «ناحیهٔ مشکل» تو می‌تواند کجا باشد. آیا فاصلهٔ بین عرضه و «ناحیهٔ مشکل» حداقل 1/1 rr است؟ اگر بله، خوب است؛ اگر نه، از معامله بگذر. به نقطه تصمیم برای استاپ‌هایت هم نگاه کن و مطمئن شو با اندازهٔ استاپت جور درمی‌آید.
