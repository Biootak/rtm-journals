Thought I would take a crack at this, as it was a good exercise for me as well. Here's my take, which include some adaptations I've made for my own trading: 1) First FTR: Validly identified. However I look for the RBR that every DBR reacts to (The DBR in this case being your FTR). This RBR was not engulfed where you marked, so I wouldn't consider the next level an FTR. And the RBR bounced price nicely to the next supply on this timeframe.

2) If you follow/agree with the logic of #1, then the 2nd FTR marked is not an actual FTR. And it also seems as part of a compression, so I wouldnt look to trade it, nor would I look for an engulf of the level to ID the next FTR. 3) 3rd FTR identified not valid as per my rules because of statements in #2. But when the compression is broken a little higher, I see a nice FL created which bounces price a couple of times in that flag (above where you marked your 3rd ftr). Thats a nice trade.

Also looking to the right, when that FL breaks, looks like a new FL is formed and a nice bounce from it when revisited.

4) Your fourth FTR would not be an FTR, even if the first 3 were valid, because a lower low was not made.

5) Because of #4, your 5th FTR was not valid.

It really is a pain in the A because if you identify one thing wrong, it can lead to a series of incorrect levels.

Also - assuming all were identified correctly, it also pays to look at the quality of the level. So each FTR is pretty much a RBD or DBR. Look at each on your timeframe, and see which of these you see a clear drop, basing, then rally (or vice versa). It's really only the 3rd FTR which could meet this criteria. Alot of them CP into or out of the base which isnt great. And the 3rd one likely didnt react because it wasnt actually an FTR.

Hope this helps! This is just my take, though. Would love to hear others' input.
