I think the SPAZ type works only if there is nothing important on the left, like a broken key level.

Because the break of a key level makes it an FTR/FL.

Also the reason for ITs creating stacked S/D is to pick up orders on the
 way and on return to fake out all classic S/D and S/R traders.

Think about it this way, if ITs created those stacked demand/supply
bases to fill their orders on the way, this means that on return there
is little possibility of unfilled orders still left there. This is
especially true for compression PAZs.

That's why price needs to break only the last/first demand just before the CPAZ or SPAZ to make it through the entire zone.

So if there are still unfilled orders then they are likely there at the tip (entrance of PAZ) and not inside that consumed zone.

Right, PAZs are just a piece of the puzzle like everything else.

This is something that I think is the key to understand:

1. Some FL/FTRs work but many don't, so this concept is not enough on its own

2. Some engulfs work but many don't, so this concept is not enough on its own

3. PAZs (all types) work but many don't, so this concept is not enough on its own

4. QM/Diamond patterns work but many don't, so this concept is not enough on its own

5. Compression is sometimes a reversal sign but not always (LQ after compression is common)

6. S/D levels work but many don't

7. FO levels sometimes work but many don't

... I think it is clear where this is leading to.

Perhaps this is exactly what IF means by saying that we need to make
that final juxtaposition, where we see those concepts all at the same
time. Often there is this common question in the homework section: "Why
did that or this FTR/FL/CP/... fail?" Because it was never meant to work
 that way.

Looking at the charts, there are almost dead sure setups that present
itself only if multiple of the above RTM concepts appear together in the
 same setup.

For example: at HTF supply, a trick is played (FO) then at the right
shoulder of the trick level price forms an FTR/FL which happens to be a
QM level also. Price meanwhile broke a lower FL and starts to retrace to
 the QM level. On approach price is starting to compress (making a
CPAZ). Once price hits the QM FTR level at the FO key level at HTF
supply price bounces and breaks into the CPAZ. If price now retraces
back to the origin for a second chance entry then you have one of those
almost dead sure trades

درست است، PAZ هم مثل هر چیز دیگری فقط یک تکه از پازل است.

این چیزی است که فکر می‌کنم کلید فهمیدن است:

1. بعضی FL/FTR‌ها جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه، پس این مفهوم به‌تنهایی کافی نیست

2. بعضی اینگالف‌ها جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه، پس این مفهوم به‌تنهایی کافی نیست

3. PAZ‌ها (همهٔ انواع) جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه، پس این مفهوم به‌تنهایی کافی نیست

4. الگوهای QM/دایموند جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه، پس این مفهوم به‌تنهایی کافی نیست

5. فشردگی گاهی نشانهٔ بازگشت است ولی نه همیشه (LQ بعد از فشردگی رایج است)

6. سطوح S/D جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه

7. سطوح FO گاهی جواب می‌دهند ولی خیلی‌ها نه

... فکر می‌کنم واضح است که این به کجا می‌رسد.

به نمودارها که نگاه می‌کنی، ستاپ‌هایی هست که تقریباً صددرصدی‌اند و فقط وقتی پیش می‌آیند که چند مورد از مفاهیم بالا همزمان در یک ستاپ جمع شوند.
