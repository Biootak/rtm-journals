Your points 1, 2 and 3 are all part of the stacked demand. In general
when price gets into stacked demand, it normally wants to go through it
to the top or bottom level, in this case the bottom level. It is not the
 same as compression as compression takes out remaining orders while
stacked demand still have orders hiding away. But if you think about it
logically it will be more profitable to get to the cheapest or most
expensive level to trade from...

...you can see from base 1 that price takes off and comes back to
re-test the base, these orders have now been filled and it is this point
 I want to see broken to get into the stacked demand below. This happens
 as you can see at the circle, and the entry is simply a supply/QM
level.

You can see now that price returned to the last base in the stacked demand, which is also a flip zone and took off very nicely.

EN: Ahhhh....sweet sweet eureka comes yet again...Thank you!

So yes PAZ is any price action or price structure on the chart that
shows all opposing levels on the chart have been taken out at some
point, and price should cut right through to the last untouched level.
Compression is the simplest to understand because the last sentence is
all compression is about. But PAZ could take on an infinite number of
looks as long as it shows the remaining orders in all opposing levels
have been filled.

But the PAZ can never include the most recent level because you would
not expect price to just cut right through it. So you mark all the
violated levels up to the most recent, then when the most recent gets
violated it shows that the buyers/sellers are no longer interested in
holding price up/down. Thats when you enter on what ever reaction that
most recent level caused.
