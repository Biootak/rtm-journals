In Forex trading there is much less regulation than in equities for
example. So with fewer rules, the banks will use every tool at their
disposal to take our money or in this case not allow you into a trade
that will cost them money.

During every news release they have an excuse to widen the spread. The
more important the news the better the excuse they have. If you are
executing a long position, you need to be aware that these positions are
 even harder to catch because they normally widen the ask. They do this
 because our charts print based on the bid price and it is better to
hide their manipulation.

Here is a chart of today I picked. You can see the spread goes as high
as 6 pips at times with this broker. Granted this is daily rollover
when it gets this high. However the spread increases multiple times a
day---news releases, equity market openings, market closings, slower
times, faster times, etc, etc, etc.

Now here is another chart for you. This is a screenshot I took when I got 360
 pips of slippage on USDCAD. I, of course, contacted the broker and
their answer as always is that it is not them but the liquidity
providers. Don't be fooled.

* Be aware when trading around things like Central Bank Rate announcements.

* Beware trading around end of day rollover or even worse end of week.

* Understand the more "exotic" a pair the wider the spread can go
without warning. NZD normally has higher spread than EUR and in some
cases, IT will go absolutely nuts on the spread around things like end
of day rollover.

* Recognize that long trades at fantastic areas might need an extra pip or two to get filled.

I think in scenario 1 your trade is dead the moment ask price widens 30 pips

Scenario 2 will not execute the trade

Explanation: those price is not related to your condition but other party's condition.

Bid is someone willing to pay you (when you sell)

Ask is someone willing to offer to you (when you buy)

Simplified:

When you look for SL placement. Watch out for line closer to you.

Buy execute - look out for ask line hitting your SL

Sell execute - look out for bid line hitting your SL

When setting limit orders - watch the line which is further from you

For sell limits the bid line has to touch your limit line

For buy limit your ask line has to touch your limit line

In conclusion look for line which is disadvantageous to u.
