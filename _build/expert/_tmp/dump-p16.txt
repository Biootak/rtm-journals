One thing that really helped me in the last couple of months was to make sure all of my trades had enough room for profit. I had to learn the hard way after suffering several losses that there is no need to force a trade when there is no free 'space' for price to move and at least reach a decent first target. Sure, sometimes price blows right through a zone but a good chunk of the time, orders have to get absorbed first. I would even say that space is the most important thing to look for in a possible trade. Observe some of the trades that the senior members have posted and look at how obvious a lot of their trades look. Most times you will notice, they look so appealing because they had enough room for profit. Also, scrub trough the PIE tread on FF and look at Red's charts. Pretty much all of his trades have clear room for profit and he usually takes off 2/3 of his position at the first major obstacle (usually an SR/ flip zone) and closes out the rest of the position at the source/ origin of the move.

Also, here is an acronym I developed that has helped me focus on the important things to look for in a trade. The acronym is

and the breakdown is as

STTAR

follows:

Make sure there is room for profit and enough space to get decent a RRR

SPACE: at your first target.

Try to trade during the corresponding hot spots for your pair. In other

TIMING: words, try to time your trades during the major market opens for the corresponding pair you are trading (i.e. trade the Yen pairs during the Asian session, trade the Euro pairs during the London session etc...) You will also notice that a lot of fake outs and direction changes form during session changes (opens and closes) and news events..

The trade is either counter trend, trend following, or neutral (in the case

TREND: of a ranging market). This matters a lot when it comes to how you want to manage the trade. In counter trend situations, I am much more willing to take full profit at my first target and be aggressive with my trailing stop. In trend following trades, I am open to holding on to my trades for more profit and I allow more lenience for pullbacks. I tend to treat ranging market trades like counter trend trades i.e. be aggressive with the stop trailing and profit taking.. Now of course the market is fractal and trends can be different on different TFs, but I tend to look at the HTFs for the ultimate gauge on what the trend is (4hr, daily, weekly....) But if you are a 1min scalper per say, then you could just use the 1hr charts as your trend curve etc...

Is price moving in a parabolic, steady, or compressed manner

APPROACH: toward your trade zone. If it is moving parabolic toward your zone of interest, then sometimes the momentum is unsustainable. Other times, the overzealous momentum of a parabolic move can blow right through zones. IMHO, It is important to try to use confirmation trades when price is moving toward your zone of interest in a parabolic fashion. The good thing about parabolic approaches, is that they give you ample space/ room for profit if the LTF confirmation gives you the signal to take the trade. If price is moving in a healthy, steady manner toward your zone and especially if it is building a base right before the zone (remember the importance of 'room for profit'), this is another time to use caution when looking for a trade because the PA shows signs of a healthy trend. I also tend to wait for LTF confirmation in these scenarios. If price is compresses into your zone, then you can usually put on a limit order and let her rip IMO.

Did your zone cause a LTF engulf and in what manner did it do so. Was

Reaction: it a fast powerful rejection, or a weak limp one. If the rejection was weak, perhaps price wants to go higher. Look our for reactions that occur before the bounds of the zone are reached. Institutions LOVE to use these as fake out levels to get cheap easy orders.

If you think about it, as long as these 5 principles all check out, then you have a very good chance of having a profitable trade. They don't all have to check out for every trade, but the more of them that do, the better chance your trade has of winning ( I personally recommend that at least 3 out of the 5 principles check out in every trade with the idea of 'space' being the constant that has to be present every time).

Sorry for the long explanations to my STTAR acronym as I'm sure you already know most of this information but I just wanted to show you something that has helped me quickly concentrate my mind on the important things to look for before taking a trade.

Hope this helps, SyT
