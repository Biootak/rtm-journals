I ponder the same questions PS, especially when I hit a long run of losses. Here's what I think in red.

projectsagana wrote: Why does sometimes current price action seems so clear and sometimes don't? In my opinion, before even placing a trade, it's about making sure you have a plan that identifies the bare minimum of variables such as; reversal possibilities, obstacles and targets in multiple time-frames. If you're forcing a trade for the sake of having something on, you probably haven't done the requisite preparation, and you will not make the right decisions when you need to on entry, trade management, or exit possibilities. Disaster!

Is this just a matter of state of mind? Like being in the zone? Look I am not a psychologist so take my words with a grain of salt, but being in the right state of mind is a direct consequence of being adequately prepared to trade, confidence stems from preparedness, and with confidence you establish a winning attitude. You're prepared by establishing 'best practice'. Best practice being, having a trading plan with a system you trust, recognising you're in a healthy mindset (not stressed), not allowing outside influences effect your judgement. Does this make sense?

I've definitely had moments when I see price happen ahead of time and were able to take advantage of that. Definitely, sometimes it'll happen naturally, and you won't even know it until after the fact and you have somehow magically added 20% to your account without batting an eyelid. It'll be different for everyone but it is worthwhile reflecting on your own personal habit and what you did right in correlation to how the market behaved during that time to understand how you can get yourself in that zone time and time again.

And others not so much. Hmmm... My goal is be able to read the market and see the ITs intentions clearly ALL THE TIME. Yep, yep and yep. Been there. Still end up there after ridiculous winning streaks. The self doubt creeps in and you trust your plan less, you deviate from your system, you turn into a loose cannon. Always do your analysis from the top down, HIGHER time frame. How often do look at a daily chart? I hope every day! One time-frame analysis will have you scurrying away into a corner when you watch FTR after FTR fail.

Anyone have this phase? You betcha!
