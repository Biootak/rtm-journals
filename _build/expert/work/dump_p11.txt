Now that I am practicing S/R flips, I wonder if the source of the move (that also contains the DP) is the FL that needs to be EG in order for a setup or QM to be valid.

Here is an example on E/U. It's just a 1-min chart, but it is a good example of what would trick me. At first glance (to me) it looks like a QM setup. But the source of the move that broke the S/R has not been engulfed, so it is not valid.

But then there is the complication of scale. Same chart, but on a 5 min time frame (a few mins have passed since I took the screen shot of the 1 min chart, so price has progressed a bit). On the 5 min chart, it looks like an engulf would require price to break out of a larger FL. Very subjective, very frustrating. I'd say when in doubt go with the higher time frame.

I'll keep practicing these to see if I am on the right track.

And then there is the further complication (once you determine which FL needs to be broken to signal the engulf) of judging whether the price break is really an engulf or just a fakeout. My understanding is it depends on where price breaks to. Price that breaks in to an important zone containing orders (FL, FTR, S/R Flip etc...) is likely a F/O. So in the case below (EUR-USD) price has finally broken the 5 min source, however it is clear price also tagged a S/R zone creating an S/R flip. So do we have an engulf or a F/O? And how would we judge this in real time without waiting for PA to form and make it obvious in hindsight?
