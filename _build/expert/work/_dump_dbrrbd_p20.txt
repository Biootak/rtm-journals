I make reference to concerns about the observations in items A2, A4 and A5. They occur on different time-frames:

1. A2 is on the H4 (first image, lower chart). I draw a tail on the side nearest price so I can check the Price Action history at this level on longer time-frames. The obvious thought is that the more time price spends away from a level, and the greater distance it travels, the more out of balance it is and the more likely that price will react on its return (effectively mean-reversion). I also check reactions to the level, particularly which side price was on just before the zone I'm investigating - I much prefer it to be on the opposite side, indicating that I am looking at a fresh zone (a flip). As I mentioned before I don't like obvious lines of S or R or trend, they are the chart equivalent of the luminescent beacon hanging in front of the Anglerfish's mouth. Here, the last reaction was on the same side, not so attractive 2. A4 is on the H1 chart (second image, upper chart). I check to see how convincingly price moves away from the zone I'm interested in. I like to see long and strong candles with little overlap, and I like to see local opposition levels being taken out easily, preferably to a higher low or a lower high - it shows intent. Here price moves away strongly enough, breaks a small Demand level but turns back at an area that I would consider weak. Again, not so attractive 3. A5 is on the M5 chart (second image, lower chart).
