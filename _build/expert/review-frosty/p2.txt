FTR Homework #10: Another FTR homework and what I think I have learned more about is that FTRs that gets tested and bounced more than a few times are places where ITs are either buying or selling. When these FTRs are engulfed, price then will try to reach the target, which, in my current opinion & understanding, the next origin of a move on the opposite side. For example, an FTR where price bounced down (short) from a few times, when engulfed, will create an FTR and now this FTR will become the new place where the ITs will load up on longs until the most extreme sets of orders in the next origin of move on the previous down leg has been touched. The logic applies in reverse for shorts.

From the 3 charts from FTR homework #9, I learned that the target for long orders are short orders, and vice versa. After much thinking then the extreme sets of clean orders in the origin DBR/RBD of the previous move up or down must have been the target because this is where the ITs started either buying or selling, i.e. shows their decision. This extreme sets of orders, I believe, is the MPL, which can be seen as a QM level that has not been touched yet.

Bro Staurum actually pointed this out on the CHF/JPY chart onpost #29 marked with "g". When there is no more orders left there, a new set of orders is coming in.

After reading up on QMs and after reading many QM-related posts, I have learned a few things: 1. QMs must occur at HTF important zones for them to be considered tradeable. No zone, no QM (even if it looks like one)

2. Ignored left shoulder (LS) level are a good place for a bounce on the way back.
