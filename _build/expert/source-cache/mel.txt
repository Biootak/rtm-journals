I made everything complicated, I was overthinking! I was just stupid!Periode! Yup, there is a big difference between fighting price and following it. For me, the best way to follow price is by chunking it down and trying to see it as simply as I can. Watching the Hs, Ls, the ranges that form from them and the movement in and around those ranges. For me boxes are the foundation to my trading as they put a frame to what I am viewing. What most learn about trading is levels and PA. The bigger piece of the picture is how price MOVES. To get a feel for this, open up any chart on the 1h and get a pen and paper out. Draw out the main Hs and Ls. Then draw out boxes to chunk it into individual bits. Look at how price moves in and around those Hs / Ls and the boxes. You will see that price has a few standard characteristics and they are incredibly simple. Usually when price does 'A' it then does 'B'. For example, if price establishes a H/L with a retest, it will usually fill the range back or beyond the previous H/L. These things are not patterns, they are dynamics. Hs and Ls are the most essential part of the chart. Every day there is a H and L that is formed and there are many Hs and Ls that form in between them. Again with your pen and paper, divide the price out in time periods and see how they move during different sessions and days of the week. Get curious.. why does it do that? It took me a few weeks of doing this to get to grips with it. Check this box to be notified of replies to this topic. Note: BBcode and smileys are still usable. I have been introduced to trading in 2008, didn't had any succes in the first years. I didn't want to become a Trader when I was a little girl, in fact I didn't even know that this kind of activity existed before. I guess, it was the lack of succes that I had in the first years that was what kept me going. This is what triggered my intrest to get to know more about the market. I had this feeling that it was possible to become succesfull in trading and to learn everything about how the market worked, inside out. It has been a long journey since then, as I tested so many different things that did not worked. In fact, it was me, that did not knew how to read the market. Eventually, after so many years I found a way, I finally discovered the true beauty of the market and now that I know,I am certainly not going to give up and stop trading.... :) Trading Style & Strategy I consider myslef to be a Day/Swing Trader but I also take some scalps here and there if a good opportunity present itself. I am a supply and demand trader but I also look at PA. I do a weekly analysis every sunday for the coming week and I also adjust or modify my plan if needed every day, before London open. I like to trader higher TF supply and demand zones but I do like to look for lower TF fresh zones within the higher TF ones. I mainly trade the EU but I may also check other pairs once in awhile. I do not trade the Asian session. I prefer to trade from London open untill London close. Money Management: I will never risk more then 2% per trade. If I do have 3 losses in a row, I will stop trading for the day and I will analyze why the trades failed. -My r:r per trade need to be at least 1/1 , otherwise I will not take the trade. If I take a trade at a supply zone, I would prefer to exit at the demand zone and vice versa. -For my stop loss, I use 2% risk but I will look at LTF charts to look for the last 2 decision points, if thos are broken (against me) then I will exit the trade manually and I will take the loss. -My main focus is to preserve my capital. -In terms of return, my daily minimum goal is 20 pips gain. I normally do not want to set a fix minimum return in terms of pips because I want to take what the market wants to give me (if it's more fine, if less fine too) but since I have to set some goals for myself, I won't go for too much pips. 20 pips seems to be fair enough. Points to be careful about : -Don't trade what you think, but trade what you see. Buy at demand and sell at supply! -Pay attention to higher TF zones, as those are more powerfull than LTF ones. -Always try to improve yourself! Explain how you do your analysis and what you look for. 1)Look at where price is NOW, go ahead and mark all the supply zones that are above current price and all the demand zones that are under current price on 1h up to a monthly chart. 2) Mark in a specific color which of this higher TF zones are Fresh ones. 3) Find lower TF fresh supply and demand zones that are located in the higher TF zones from point 1 & 2. 4)Now that you have the zones marked take another chart and mark last day, week and month highs and lows. 5) Back to point 1, 2 & 3. On a lower TF, identify where price is now and try to locate where the previous decision points are (to the upside and to the downside). 6) Pre-entry, when price does approach the zones that you have marked, look at how it approches exactly. Is it compressing towards the supply or demand zone? If yes, then this is a plus point. 7) Look at point 6, if we are approaching a supply for example, look at where you first "problem area " can be. Is the distance between the supply and the "problem area" at least 1/1 rr? If yes, then ok, if not pass the trade. Look also at decision point for your stops, make sure it fits your stop size. About the entry,I want to look at PA. My prefered entry is this: Let's assume we are in a higher TF supply zone, I like to nr 1 see price cp towards it at least on a LTF chart, then usually price start to flag under this supply ( on a LTF) , what I like to see is a move away from this flag, then a break a minor demand base (on a LTF) and a retest of the fresh formed supply flag. Let's assume I took a similar trade, the next thing I want to do in order to stay in the trade is to look for break of the next minor demand lows/decision point.If price is unable to break the lows, then it might move in the opposite direction, so I'll look to exit the trade. I think that this is it. I will update this post if somthing changes in my trading plan. Mel EDIT August 16 2012 Adding a Rule to my plan -Identify swap levels From 15 min TF and higher. What I want to see is a break of a supply and then a well defined base,(RBR)and vice versa for demand (DBD). If there is a break of a supply/demand zone without any clear base around the break, then I won't trade the first visit of the level. The base, if there is one, would have to be a well defined one wich will not be tested with any wicks and the move away from it will will have to be clear and strong. If I find a potential good swap level that fits the above "rules" I'll look at the chart to see where my first area of "trouble" is (DP, S/D). The distance between the Swap and any potential "trouble" area should be at leat 1:1 rr , otherwise I'll pass the trade and move on dot com. Check this box to be notified of replies to this topic. For me, the best way to follow price is by chunking it down and trying to see it as simply as I can. Watching the Hs, Ls, the ranges that form from them and the movement in and around those ranges. For me boxes are the foundation to my trading as they put a frame to what I am viewing. What most learn about trading is levels and PA. The bigger piece of the picture is how price MOVES. To get a feel for this, open up any chart on the 1h and get a pen and paper out. Draw out the main Hs and Ls. Then draw out boxes to chunk it into individual bits. Look at how price moves in and around those Hs / Ls and the boxes. You will see that price has a few standard characteristics and they are incredibly simple. Usually when price does 'A' it then does 'B'. For example, if price establishes a H/L with a retest, it will usually fill the range back or beyond the previous H/L. These things are not patterns, they are dynamics. Hs and Ls are the most essential part of the chart. Every day there is a H and L that is formed and there are many Hs and Ls that form in between them. Again with your pen and paper, divide the price out in time periods and see how they move during different sessions and days of the week. Get curious.. why does it do that? For me boxes are the foundation to my trading as they put a frame to what I am viewing. What most learn about trading is levels and PA. The bigger piece of the picture is how price MOVES. To get a feel for this, open up any chart on the 1h and get a pen and paper out. Draw out the main Hs and Ls. Then draw out boxes to chunk it into individual bits. Look at how price moves in and around those Hs / Ls and the boxes. You will see that price has a few standard characteristics and they are incredibly simple. Usually when price does 'A' it then does 'B'. For example, if price establishes a H/L with a retest, it will usually fill the range back or beyond the previous H/L. These things are not patterns, they are dynamics. Hs and Ls are the most essential part of the chart. Every day there is a H and L that is formed and there are many Hs and Ls that form in between them. Again with your pen and paper, divide the price out in time periods and see how they move during different sessions and days of the week. Get curious.. why does it do that? I have been introduced to trading in 2008, didn't had any succes in the first years. I didn't want to become a Trader when I was a little girl, in fact I didn't even know that this kind of activity existed before. I guess, it was the lack of succes that I had in the first years that was what kept me going. This is what triggered my intrest to get to know more about the market. I had this feeling that it was possible to become succesfull in trading and to learn everything about how the market worked, inside out. It has been a long journey since then, as I tested so many different things that did not worked. In fact, it was me, that did not knew how to read the market. Eventually, after so many years I found a way, I finally discovered the true beauty of the market and now that I know,I am certainly not going to give up and stop trading.... :) Trading Style & Strategy I consider myslef to be a Day/Swing Trader but I also take some scalps here and there if a good opportunity present itself. I am a supply and demand trader but I also look at PA. I do a weekly analysis every sunday for the coming week and I also adjust or modify my plan if needed every day, before London open. I like to trader higher TF supply and demand zones but I do like to look for lower TF fresh zones within the higher TF ones. I mainly trade the EU but I may also check other pairs once in awhile. I do not trade the Asian session. I prefer to trade from London open untill London close. Money Management: I will never risk more then 2% per trade. If I do have 3 losses in a row, I will stop trading for the day and I will analyze why the trades failed. -My r:r per trade need to be at least 1/1 , otherwise I will not take the trade. If I take a trade at a supply zone, I would prefer to exit at the demand zone and vice versa. -For my stop loss, I use 2% risk but I will look at LTF charts to look for the last 2 decision points, if thos are broken (against me) then I will exit the trade manually and I will take the loss. -My main focus is to preserve my capital. -In terms of return, my daily minimum goal is 20 pips gain. I normally do not want to set a fix minimum return in terms of pips because I want to take what the market wants to give me (if it's more fine, if less fine too) but since I have to set some goals for myself, I won't go for too much pips. 20 pips seems to be fair enough. Points to be careful about : -Don't trade what you think, but trade what you see. Buy at demand and sell at supply! -Pay attention to higher TF zones, as those are more powerfull than LTF ones. -Always try to improve yourself! Explain how you do your analysis and what you look for. 1)Look at where price is NOW, go ahead and mark all the supply zones that are above current price and all the demand zones that are under current price on 1h up to a monthly chart. 2) Mark in a specific color which of this higher TF zones are Fresh ones. 3) Find lower TF fresh supply and demand zones that are located in the higher TF zones from point 1 & 2. 4)Now that you have the zones marked take another chart and mark last day, week and month highs and lows. 5) Back to point 1, 2 & 3. On a lower TF, identify where price is now and try to locate where the previous decision points are (to the upside and to the downside). 6) Pre-entry, when price does approach the zones that you have marked, look at how it approches exactly. Is it compressing towards the supply or demand zone? If yes, then this is a plus point. 7) Look at point 6, if we are approaching a supply for example, look at where you first "problem area " can be. Is the distance between the supply and the "problem area" at least 1/1 rr? If yes, then ok, if not pass the trade. Look also at decision point for your stops, make sure it fits your stop size. About the entry,I want to look at PA. My prefered entry is this: Let's assume we are in a higher TF supply zone, I like to nr 1 see price cp towards it at least on a LTF chart, then usually price start to flag under this supply ( on a LTF) , what I like to see is a move away from this flag, then a break a minor demand base (on a LTF) and a retest of the fresh formed supply flag. Let's assume I took a similar trade, the next thing I want to do in order to stay in the trade is to look for break of the next minor demand lows/decision point.If price is unable to break the lows, then it might move in the opposite direction, so I'll look to exit the trade. I think that this is it. I will update this post if somthing changes in my trading plan. Mel EDIT August 16 2012 Adding a Rule to my plan -Identify swap levels From 15 min TF and higher. What I want to see is a break of a supply and then a well defined base,(RBR)and vice versa for demand (DBD). If there is a break of a supply/demand zone without any clear base around the break, then I won't trade the first visit of the level. The base, if there is one, would have to be a well defined one wich will not be tested with any wicks and the move away from it will will have to be clear and strong. If I find a potential good swap level that fits the above "rules" I'll look at the chart to see where my first area of "trouble" is (DP, S/D).
