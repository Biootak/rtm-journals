Posts: 453 Thanks man! Thank you received: AK. 1016 Hi Astrokillah, Thank you for your kind words. I too found (still sometimes find) FLs to be the most confusing part of RTM. But of course they are also the most important part, lol. Back in the past IF talked about FL, FTR, DPs, etc. But now we reference all of them as FL because people get hung up on the terminology. It is our job to identify the good FLs. The ones that were formed from breaking a significant barrier like S/R or a past FL of significance (or sometimes the ones that preceded the breaking of the significant level-- DPs). If we accept that all these DPs, FTR, FL,etc can all be called FLs. Then the only other thing is what is in between the FLs which are the PAZs. So the market is really just FL, PAZ, FL, PAZ, FL, PAZ, FL. To answer your question: we don't know a FL is a FL until it crosses the PAZ to the next FL and touches it. SR by itself is not a target (although it is normally formed off a past FL. If price reaches a DP of significance (lead to a break of S/R) then I would call that DP a FL and say that price hit its target. If price hits a CAP, that CAP normally forms off a previous FL and I would say that price hit its target. The times we don't consider price to have hit a target are times when price breaks a FL but doesn't completely cross the PAZ. These are your engulfs, HOME EDUCATION FORUM MORE LOGOUT FOs, BSZ, etc. These are great because these are the markers that indicate it is time to start looking for trades. Other times, price will for Contact Us example engulf a FL and pull back a lot and then eventually push to the target. These are the times were you endFAuQp with a much bigger FL.
