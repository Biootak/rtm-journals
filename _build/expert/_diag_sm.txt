Thank you for the excellent comment, I agree completely.

It did occur to me as I posted the previous diagrams that this was a gross over-simplification of how the market actually works, and it was not my intent to state the above as an immutable law of the markets - but then, every lesson in the Markepedia is along the same lines.

The theory taught here on RTM is along the lines of "If the market was a perfect model, this is how it should behave". You then go on to realise there are layers and layers of subtlety and nuance on top of all the basic building blocks, the mastering of which is what takes 1000s of hours of chart time, and enables you to make the leap from "competent but inconsistent trader" into "consistent and highly profitable trader".

However, as over-simplifications go, I believe that my second diagram is a much more useful over-simplification than the first one, and if a beginner were to adhere religiously to one price model they would be better served by using this.

As you say, the market is an inherently complex entity. The only way we can begin to make sense of it is by having a set of rules which should hold true *most* of the time. We then have to learn under what conditions they might *not* hold true, by repeated application and study of when they failed, and trying to consider *why* they failed in terms of the behaviour of ITs creating liquidity to fill large positions.

Every scale has PAZ. We are either at the edges or in the middle of a PAZ on every single scale at any given time. We have a "where we are coming from" and "where we are probably headed to" in play on every scale at the same time. The smaller scale FTR and PAZ give us a "how we are going to get to the likely destination" in terms of our larger scale PAZ. Sometimes an engulf happens, which does not play out for days or weeks, until price has RT'd all the way back to the opposite side of the PAZ on the scale on which it formed. Sometimes price only makes a shallow RT. Sometimes you believe you've seen an engulf, but it turns out to be a FO only after a lot more price action has played out, and confirms it as such. Sometimes you have what appear to be opposing engulfs in play at the same time on the same scale. Sometimes new price action plays out which invalidates your previous assumption, and you must remain flexible enough to change your mind quickly - sticking to a flawed assumption in the face of conflicting evidence is a recipe for disaster.

Really, it can be an utter nightmare for a beginner to wrap their heads around as we know, which is why we must start from a simplified model, and extrapolate exceptions and nuances from that point.

As we know, the further you are from a PAZ edge on any given scale, the lower the probabilities that price is going to behave in a logically consistent manner. As you get closer and closer to the edges of a PAZ, and factor in how price approached, what engulfs are in play etc, then the probabilities of you being able to take a successful entry at a level increase greatly - the fuzziness begins to come more sharply into focus. The greater the scale of the PAZ edge, the sharper the focus and the higher the probability of a successful trade from the level.

Over time, one realises (or I did at least), that 99% of the time the best thing to do in the market is wait. Wait until price arrives at a level in the manner you expect, when as many pre-conditions of "is this trade likely to be successful" are met. Pre-conditions include, but not limited to: - is price moving away from a valid target? - is there a supporting engulf in play? - have we had a LTF engulf of FTA coming off a HTF level? - is this FTB to this level? - is this the deepest possible RT level on this scale? - can price FO this level, and if so, where can it FO to? - how did we approach the level? - what is the scale of this level? - is there any high volatility news approaching? - can I see any LTF rejection PA from this level to confirm opposing orders going in? - is there a confluence of important SR near MPL of this zone?

The list is large, and this list is probably not exhaustive, but only when all things are taken into account can you start hitting high win rates.
